Resume & Profile

A recruiter spends under a minute on your resume. Make that minute count.

Resume checklist

  • One or two pages, simple layout, readable font.
  • A short summary at the top: who you are, what you do, what you want next.
  • Bullets that show results: "Cut report time from 4 hours to 30 minutes" beats "Responsible for reports".
  • Keywords from the job posting, used honestly, so applicant tracking systems can find you.
  • No typos. Read it aloud, then ask a friend to read it.

Online profile

  • Use a clear, friendly photo and a headline that says what you do.
  • Match your profile to your resume: same titles, same dates.
  • Ask former colleagues or managers for short recommendations.

Interview Prep

Good preparation turns nerves into confidence.

Before the interview

  • Research the company: what it does, who its customers are, recent news.
  • Re-read the job description and match it to your own examples.
  • Prepare three to five stories from your work. Use the STAR method below.
  • Prepare two or three thoughtful questions to ask them.
  • Test your camera, microphone and internet for video calls. Plan your route for in-person ones.

The STAR method

S: Situation

Set the scene in one or two sentences.

T: Task

What was your responsibility or goal?

A: Action

What did you do, step by step?

R: Result

What changed? Use numbers if you can.

After the interview

  • Send a short thank-you email within 24 hours.
  • Note what went well and what to improve for next time.

Job Search

A steady, organized search beats sending hundreds of random applications.

A simple weekly plan

  • Set a target: for example 5 quality applications and 3 conversations per week.
  • Tailor your resume to each role instead of sending the same one everywhere.
  • Tell friends, family and former colleagues what you are looking for. Many jobs are filled by referral.
  • Track every application in a simple sheet: company, role, date, contact, next step.
  • Follow up politely after one week if you have not heard back.

Where to look

  • Company career pages for places you would like to work.
  • General job boards and professional networking sites.
  • Local job centers, community groups and career fairs.

Stay motivated

Rejection is part of the process and says little about your worth. Keep learning, keep practicing, and celebrate small wins like every interview you complete.
